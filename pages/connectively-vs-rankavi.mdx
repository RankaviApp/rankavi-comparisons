## Why this comparison looks a little different

Most comparisons on this site put a live competitor side by side with Rankavi. This one is different, because Connectively no longer exists as an operating business. It was Cision's rebrand of the original Help a Reporter Out, better known as HARO. It shut down in December 2024.

We are including it anyway, because people still search for it. Understanding what HARO became before it disappeared gives useful context for anyone comparing Rankavi to today's journalist-matching tools.

## What Connectively actually was

Connectively was a journalist-source platform. Reporters posted queries, and PR pros and experts pitched themselves as sources for stories. That basic model traces back to the original HARO. Cision acquired it, rebranded it as Connectively, and eventually folded the service entirely.

Rankavi is not a journalist-matching platform at all. It sells unlinked brand mentions built specifically for AI answer engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews. The two products solved different problems even while they existed at the same time.

## Where that leaves you today

If you are searching for Connectively now, you are likely looking for what replaced it. Featured.com and Qwoted are the live successors in that same journalist-matching space. Either is a better place to look for source-pitching tools than a defunct platform.

Rankavi sits in a different category entirely, and that matters here. It is not a HARO replacement. It is a direct way to buy AI-search visibility, which is a separate need from pitching journalists.

## Price per placement

Because Connectively is discontinued, there is no current pricing to compare. While it operated, its pricing was never fully public the way a flat rate card would be. That was consistent with how Cision priced other tools in that family.

Rankavi's pricing is live and public today: $15 per mention standalone, or $12 per mention in a 50-credit bulk pack. That is a real, current number you can act on.

## Access model

Connectively's access model, back when it operated, mirrored the classic HARO structure: sign up, watch for relevant queries, and pitch. It was free for journalists and open enough for most PR pros to use directly.

Rankavi is self-serve today. You sign up, fund your account, and start ordering mentions without waiting on anyone.

## Speed to first placement

With Connectively, your outcome always depended on a journalist selecting your pitch. That could take anywhere from days to never, depending on the query. That unpredictability was part of why some users moved to Featured.com or Qwoted after the shutdown.

Rankavi does not route through anyone's inbox. You order a mention and it moves into production aimed at AI answer engines.

## AI-search focus vs traditional SEO/PR

Connectively, like HARO before it, was built entirely for traditional earned media: getting quoted in news articles and blog posts. It predates the current wave of AI answer engines by years.

Rankavi was built specifically for that newer landscape. It targets how brands get mentioned inside AI-generated answers rather than traditional news coverage.

## Scalability and contract commitment

Since Connectively no longer operates, questions about its scalability or contract terms are moot. Its model was manual pitching regardless of volume. That was never designed to scale the way a modern API product can.

Rankavi scales through bulk credit packs and API access, with no contract required and lower per-mention pricing at volume.

## Why HARO's shutdown mattered to the industry

The shutdown of Connectively closed a chapter that started years earlier with the original HARO. Many PR professionals relied on it daily. Its closure in December 2024 left a real gap in the source-pitching space. That is part of why alternatives like Featured.com, Qwoted, and Source of Sources gained attention afterward.

Rankavi was not built to fill that specific gap. It emerged from a different need entirely. That need is tied to how AI answer engines like ChatGPT and Perplexity shape what people see about a brand.

## Lessons from a discontinued tool

There is a useful lesson in Connectively's shutdown for anyone evaluating vendors today. Tools built around free, ad-hoc journalist matching can be fragile as a business model. They rarely generate direct revenue from the PR side using them. That fragility is worth weighing when you pick a long-term vendor.

Rankavi runs on a straightforward paid model: $15 per mention, dropping to $12 at bulk volume. That revenue structure is simpler to sustain than a free tool supported only by journalist goodwill and platform overhead.

## If you are migrating away from Connectively

If your team relied on Connectively before its shutdown, the next step depends on what you actually used it for. For journalist pitching, Featured.com or Qwoted are the direct replacements worth evaluating first.

If part of your goal was broader brand visibility, not just earned press quotes, that is where Rankavi becomes relevant. It addresses a need that did not fully exist when Connectively first launched. That need is visibility inside AI-generated answers, not just traditional articles.

## What Connectively's shutdown teaches about vendor risk

Any tool your business depends on can disappear. Connectively is a real example of that happening to a well-known brand. Betting a workflow entirely on one free platform carries risk. The platform owner can shut it down with little notice to the users who relied on it.

Rankavi cannot fully eliminate that risk, since every business carries some. But a paid, revenue-generating model tends to be more durable than a free one funded mainly by goodwill and overhead. That structural difference is worth weighing when picking any long-term vendor for brand visibility work.

## A brief timeline for context

HARO launched in 2008 as a simple way to connect journalists and sources. It grew into a mainstay of small business PR for over a decade. Cision acquired it and eventually rebranded it as Connectively, keeping the core pitching mechanic mostly intact. The service shut down for good in December 2024, closing out that chapter.

Rankavi launched into a different landscape, one shaped by AI answer engines rather than classic search results pages. Its product did not exist as a category when HARO first launched. AI answer engines themselves are a much newer phenomenon.
